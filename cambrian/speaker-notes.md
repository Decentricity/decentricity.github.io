# The Singularity: A Cambrian Explosion of Minds — 10-minute speaker notes

## 1 — Title (0:00–0:40)
“When people say Singularity, we usually picture one giant intelligence crossing some threshold. I think that picture may be wrong. What I see in the lab — and increasingly in the industry — looks less like one mind waking up and more like the Cambrian explosion: many kinds of minds, with different architectures, speeds, substrates and niches.”

## 2 — We expected one supermind (0:40–1:30)
“For decades our metaphors were dominated by the monolith: one bigger neural network, one AGI, one discontinuity. But today we're already building very different cognitive machines. Some are tiny reflex systems. Some are language models. Some are agents. Some borrow architecture from biology. That diversity matters.”

## 3 — The model is not the mind (1:30–2:30)
“A few months ago I gave a talk about AI sovereignty, explainability, compute and data. The thing I would emphasize even more now is that those are system questions, not model questions. Where does the system remember? Who routes decisions? What can it execute? Where does data live? What is auditable? The model is increasingly just one cognitive organ.”

## 5 — Hybrid minds (3:30–4:35)
“Once you stop insisting that the LLM must be the whole mind, the design space opens up. A rule system can be better at compliance. A reflex layer can route. A large model can synthesize. Tools can act. Memory can persist. The intelligence belongs to the architecture as much as to any component.”

“The sovereignty argument gets more interesting as the hardware requirement falls. I can run capable models on a consumer GPU, turn old Linux machines into agent boxes, and put smaller decision systems onto tiny devices. This changes who can experiment — and where intelligence can physically live.”

## 8 — The Cambrian moment (6:55–8:00)
“This is why I use the Cambrian metaphor. The Cambrian explosion wasn't one organism becoming infinitely better. It was a diversification of forms into niches. AI may be entering a similar phase: reflex minds, language minds, rule minds, embodied minds, collective minds, biologically inspired minds — all exchanging signals and composing into larger systems.”

## 9 — The Singularity, plural (8:00–9:05)
“And this is where the idea gets much bigger than today's products. If intelligence becomes portable across substrates — biological, machine, or mixed — then our descendants may include both humans and what Hans Moravec called our Mind Children. Not necessarily a replacement story. Potentially a lineage story: continuation, recombination and divergence.”

## 10 — Closing (9:05–10:00)
“So my closing claim is simple: the model is not the mind. The Singularity may not be one machine becoming godlike. It may be the point where radically different kinds of minds become cheap, composable and able to inhabit many substrates — including ours. The interesting question is no longer only: how intelligent is the model? It is: what new minds can we build when intelligence is no longer one thing?”
