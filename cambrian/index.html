The Singularity:
A Cambrian Explosion of Minds

Why the future of intelligence probably won't be one giant model.
OPEN: We usually picture the singularity as one curve going vertical: one system becomes smarter and smarter until everything changes. I want to offer a different picture. The Cambrian explosion wasn't one organism becoming infinitely good. It was a sudden diversification of forms. I think intelligence may be entering that kind of phase now.
01 · The wrong mental image

The usual singularity

One model.

One scaling curve.

One increasingly general intelligence.

What may actually happen

Many architectures.

Many substrates.

Many kinds of cognition appearing at once.

Not a ladder. An ecosystem.
The dominant story is basically a ladder: scale the same thing upward. But biology rarely works that way, and computing doesn't either. Once useful components exist, people recombine them. We are already moving from a world of individual models to systems made out of models, rules, memory, tools, sensors, and other agents.
02 · I have seen one AI cycle already

In 2007, my AI was an expert system.

Rules, knowledge bases, explicit reasoning — built to help write environmental impact assessments. Then came deep learning, transformers, RLHF, tool use, agents.
The techniques changed.
The systems question came back.
I started working with AI in college around 2007, using expert systems. At the time, intelligence meant encoding structure explicitly. The modern era went in the opposite direction: learn enormous amounts of structure implicitly. Transformers changed what was possible. But now that models are capable enough, the interesting design problem is becoming architectural again: what do we put around the model?
03 · The thesis

The model is not the mind.

ModelsPattern completion, language, perception
+
RulesFast constraints and explicit knowledge
+
MemoryIdentity, continuity, experience
+
ToolsAction in the world
+
Other mindsAgents, humans, specialized systems
A foundation model is extraordinary cognitive tissue. A mind is an architecture.
This is the part I keep coming back to in my talks about sovereign and trustworthy AI. We obsess over which model is smartest. But real deployed intelligence is already a stack. The model is one cognitive organ inside a larger architecture. Once you accept that, the design space becomes much bigger than 'which LLM wins?'.

This is where the old AI world and the new one suddenly fit together. Expert systems are still fantastic when the knowledge is explicit and you need determinism. A fast decider is great for reflexes. An LLM is great when ambiguity and generalization matter. Tools act. Memory persists. A good machine mind can route between these modes rather than forcing one giant model to imitate all of them.

They are not frontier models. That is exactly why this matters: useful cognition is becoming something you can distribute, specialize, own, and compose.
05 · The Cambrian explosion

The next step is not one successor species.

Biological mindsHumans and animals, augmented by machines
Digital mindsAgents, models, persistent synthetic identities
Hybrid mindsBio + machine loops, prosthetic cognition, BCIs
Collective mindsMulti-agent systems, human-machine organizations
Simulated mindsConnectomes and biologically inspired architectures
Mind childrenArtificial descendants carrying human culture beyond one substrate
The interesting future is diversification — and eventually intelligence that can move between biological, machine, and mixed substrates.
This is why I like the Cambrian metaphor. The singularity may be less like one alien god appearing and more like an explosion of cognitive species. Some will be biological, some digital, some hybrid, some collective. Our AI systems may become what Hans Moravec called mind children: not merely tools, but another continuation of the lineage of intelligence and culture. Far enough out, substrate itself may become a choice.
THE QUESTION
Maybe the singularity isn't one mind waking up.

Maybe it's a biosphere.
For years we asked: How intelligent is the model?
The better question may be: What kinds of minds can we build?
So I would stop asking only how smart the next model will be. That question matters, but it is only one axis. The bigger question is what kinds of minds become possible once intelligence is modular, composable, biological, digital, local, networked, fast, slow, embodied, and shared. The singularity might not be a single mind waking up. It might be a biosphere.
