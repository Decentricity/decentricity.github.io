THREE DIFFERENT QUESTIONS

What would
settle it?

This explorer helps examine the case. The available data cannot deliver a binary verdict on the buried-pyramid hypothesis.

01

Is there a layered landform?

Surface geometry and subsurface contrasts can be compared. A natural volcanic hill can also be layered. The public ~30 m surface model cannot resolve individual stone terraces.

Visualizable; origin unresolved
02

Are the buried layers built by people?

That requires securely documented construction relationships and archaeological context. A resistivity or velocity anomaly alone has multiple possible explanations.

Not established by this viewer
03

When was any construction made?

Dating organic soil and dating a construction event are different inferences. The connection to construction is the issue raised in the retraction; the authors disagree.

Context of dated samples is central

A disputed paper, kept in view.

Natawidjaja et al.’s 2023 article was retracted on 18 March 2024. The notice challenges the association between the dated soils and human construction. The authors dispute the retraction. We link both records and distinguish their proposed reconstruction from independently measured data.

SOURCE NOTES / VERSION 1

What you’re looking at.

1. Georeferenced landscape

66 × 66 native Copernicus GLO-30 elevation posts form the mesh. Two AWS 2021-release geocells (S07 E107 and S08 E107) are cropped on their original grid, with no new elevation samples. Triangles connect neighboring posts for display. The DSM includes vegetation and infrastructure; it is not bare earth. Elevations use EGM2008. Dataset & license ↗

The black reference marker is the Indonesian heritage registry point, not a surveyed summit or site boundary. The grid uses approximate local east/north metres. Vertical scale defaults to 1× and can be exaggerated explicitly.

2. GEDI locations, not underground lidar

117 shots were retrieved from GFZ’s processed public V2 GEDI database on 11 November 2026, covering four dates. Three are within 100 m and eight within 250 m of the registry point. This is not an exhaustive census of NASA observations: the ingestion and filtering of the GFZ database need further audit. All retrieved shots have quality_flag=1 and degrade_flag=0; sensitivity exceeds 0.90. Flags do not guarantee terrain accuracy on steep slopes.

Rings have approximately 25 m ground diameter and are draped onto the DSM for location only. Raw elev_lowestmode values are relative to the reference ellipsoid. They are shown in the inspector and CSV but not mixed into EGM2008 terrain heights. NASA GEDI L2A ↗ · GFZ database ↗ · gediDB software citation ↗

3. Underground hypothesis model

The model is an original schematic reading of Figure 14 and Table 1 of the 2023 paper. Four nested, faceted envelopes convey layer order; their shapes and horizontal extents are visualization choices. They are not digitized survey surfaces, are not georeferenced to the DSM, and are not evidence of construction. Layer spacing and cutaway controls change the illustration, not the data. No archaeological age is assigned to the meshes.

The optional magenta wireframe illustrates the authors’ suggested main chamber dimensions of approximately 10 × 10 × 15 m (Section 3.6.2). Its location is illustrative. It does not represent a verified cavity or an independent inversion. Original radar, resistivity, and seismic figures retain their published scales in the figure viewer; none is extruded into a purported measured 3D volume.

4. Assessing the hypothesis

The explorer provides evidence access and comparison, not a proof or disproof. Geological structure, human modification, and construction age require different evidence. The paper’s retraction and the authors’ disagreement are both disclosed. Reanalysis would require raw geophysical data, survey coordinates, acquisition and inversion settings, borehole logs, and archaeological sample contexts. No buried-pyramid verdict follows from this surface dataset.
