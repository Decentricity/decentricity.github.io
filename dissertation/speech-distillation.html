Idea 003 · Model distillation · Audio-native learning

CAN A VOICE MODEL TEACH ANOTHER VOICE MODEL?

Investigating black-box speech-to-speech knowledge transfer: can a student learn natural vocal expression from generated audio without receiving text transcripts or teacher parameters?

The core question: Is expressive speech itself a sufficient supervisory signal for training a smaller audio-native student?

Two distinct tasks

Voice-quality distillation transfers timbre, cadence, emotion and pronunciation from synthetic audio. Conversational distillation additionally requires paired input/output dialogue, timing and turn-taking; isolated speech clips cannot teach how to respond appropriately to a partner.

Research questions

  1. How much generated speech is needed to reproduce a teacher's speaker identity, prosody and expressiveness?
  2. Can an audio-only student approximate a teacher's responses to unseen conversational inputs?
  3. How does teacher diversity, sampling strategy and synthetic-audio quality affect generalization?
  4. Can a small student match selected qualities of a larger teacher with fewer inference resources?
  5. What kinds of artifacts, mode collapse and errors propagate through synthetic audio?
  6. How do audio-only training and text-assisted baselines compare at matched data and compute budgets?

Experimental program

01 / Legal teacher baseline

Start with an open-weight or explicitly licensed speech generator whose outputs may be used for student training. Review usage rights before testing commercial APIs.

02 / Synthetic corpus

Generate diverse prompts, emotions, dialogue turns, accents and speaking rates. Record teacher model version, sampling settings and provenance.

03 / Audio-native student

Train a student on waveform or codec-token inputs and outputs; avoid transcripts in the target architecture. Include ordinary TTS and audio-text pipelines as baselines.

04 / Rigorous evaluation

Measure perceptual naturalness, speaker similarity, latency, expressive range, unseen-dialogue coherence, cost per generated minute and robustness.

Data and compute ladder

Prototype at 10, 50 and 100 generated hours before scaling to 500–1,000 hours. Track GPU-hours, dataset-generation costs, storage and quality-control time. These are study design points, not claims that any particular quantity guarantees a successful model.

Important boundaries

Black-box extraction may violate provider terms or contractual restrictions even when technically feasible. The research should prioritize permitted outputs, openly licensed models and reproducible experiments. Model extraction capability should not be confused with a legal right to commercialize a derivative.

Research contribution

A benchmark and method for quantifying how well audio-only student models can inherit expressive and conversational properties from synthetic speech, including failure modes and data-efficiency curves. This is related to—but distinct from—the single-person conversational identity project.
